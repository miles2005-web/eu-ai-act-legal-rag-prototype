# EU Data Act Corpus Integration Plan

## 1. Objective and integration boundary

This plan adds the official English text of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 to the assessment evidence corpus using the existing legal source catalog and `CorpusMetadataV2`. The work is an incremental corpus build, not a retrieval redesign.

The target path is:

```text
Official Data Act text
        -> verified parsed text
        -> structure-aware legal chunks
        -> metadata-v2 records
        -> vector-store export
        -> LegalEvidenceRetriever
        -> Evidence objects
        -> Industrial AI assessment report
```

The existing AI Act `vector_store.json` and its legacy evidence IDs must remain readable throughout the migration. Data Act records should use metadata v2 from their first published build; they should not depend on filename fallback for legal identity.

## 2. Source acquisition

### 2.1 Authoritative source

Acquire the English Official Journal text of **Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 December 2023 on harmonised rules on fair access to and use of data (Data Act)** from the official [EUR-Lex ELI record](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj).

Use the Official Journal version as the initial corpus version. Do not substitute commentary, unofficial consolidations, summaries, or guidance for the binding instrument. If a later consolidated text is introduced, treat it as a new `document_version` and rebuild its stable evidence IDs rather than silently replacing the original text.

### 2.2 Acquisition record

The build manifest should record at least:

- acquisition timestamp;
- official URI;
- requested language (`en`);
- source format and filename;
- source-file SHA-256 checksum;
- parsed-text SHA-256 checksum;
- catalog version; and
- whether the source is Official Journal or consolidated text.

The downloaded source and generated corpus artifacts should remain outside application code. A human should verify the title, regulation number, language, Article count, and final provisions before publication.

## 3. Required metadata

### 3.1 Catalog identity

Use the existing catalog entry as the authoritative source identity:

| Field | Data Act value |
|---|---|
| `instrument_id` | `EU_DATA_ACT` |
| title | `EU Data Act` |
| regulation number | `Regulation (EU) 2023/2854` |
| authority level | `binding_legislation` |
| official URI | `http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj` |
| jurisdiction | `EU` |
| language | `en` |

Before building, confirm the acquired filename resolves to `EU_DATA_ACT` through a catalog alias. The filename is provenance, not the legal-source identity.

### 3.2 Metadata-v2 record contract

Every exported Data Act chunk must declare `metadata_schema_version: "2.0.0"` and contain the complete `CorpusMetadataV2` fields:

- `instrument_id`: always `EU_DATA_ACT`;
- `document_version`: a single, explicit identifier for the exact official text represented by the build;
- `canonical_citation`: the smallest reliable legal location represented by the chunk;
- `authority_level`: `binding_legislation`;
- `source_record_id`: deterministic chunk identity generated by the v2 model; and
- `stable_evidence_id`: content-based evidence identity generated by the v2 model.

Recommended initial `document_version` is `Regulation (EU) 2023/2854`, matching the current catalog. A more precise Official Journal version label may be adopted later only through a deliberate catalog and corpus migration.

The stable evidence ID must be generated from:

```text
instrument_id
+ document_version
+ canonical_citation
+ normalized excerpt hash
```

It must never be derived from global chunk order, vector position, or a mutable filename.

### 3.3 Structural and provenance metadata

In addition to the required v2 contract, retain available structural fields:

```text
chapter_number
chapter_heading
section_number
section_heading
article_number
article_title
paragraph_number
point_label
parent_citation
source
official_uri
language
```

These fields support auditing and future retrieval improvements, but they must not replace the canonical v2 fields consumed by Evidence conversion.

## 4. Chunking requirements

### 4.1 Article boundaries

- Begin a new legal unit at every Article heading.
- Preserve the Article number and title.
- Do not combine text from different Articles in one chunk.
- Use `Article N` as the minimum fallback citation when finer structure cannot be established reliably.

### 4.2 Paragraph boundaries

- Preserve numbered paragraphs such as `(1)` and `(2)` beneath their parent Article.
- Prefer one paragraph per chunk where length permits.
- Construct atomic citations such as `Article 4(1)`.
- If a long paragraph must be split for size, every fragment retains the same canonical citation, while its excerpt produces a distinct stable evidence ID.

### 4.3 Point boundaries

- Preserve lettered and numbered points under their paragraph.
- Construct citations such as `Article 4(1)(a)` only when the hierarchy is explicit in the source text.
- Do not infer a point from an internal cross-reference.
- Avoid range citations in chunk identity; represent `Article 2(5)` and `Article 2(6)` as separate records.

### 4.4 Definitions

Definitions require special handling because several independently retrievable concepts may occur within one definitions Article.

- Create a separate chunk for each numbered definition.
- Preserve the defined term in `article_title` or an additional descriptive metadata field.
- Use the provision location as the canonical citation, for example `Article 2(5)`; do not use the term alone as the canonical citation.
- Retain enough text to include both the defined term and its complete definition.

### 4.5 Text integrity

- Preserve legally meaningful punctuation, numbering, headings, and defined terms.
- Normalize whitespace consistently with `normalize_evidence_excerpt()` before generating IDs.
- Do not insert summaries or LLM-generated text into authoritative excerpts.
- Validate that no chunk crosses recital, Article, Annex, or final-provision boundaries.
- Keep recitals separate from operative Articles if recitals are included in the initial corpus.

## 5. Incremental build process

### Phase 1 — Parse

1. Acquire and checksum the official source.
2. Extract the English text with the existing supported parser or prepare a faithful official-text input.
3. Normalize page artifacts without altering legal numbering.
4. Write a parsed-source manifest connecting the output to `EU_DATA_ACT`.
5. Spot-check the title, recitals, definitions, relevant access provisions, and final Article.

Exit criterion: the parsed text is complete, ordered, attributable to the official source, and reproducible from the recorded input.

### Phase 2 — Chunk

1. Run the existing structure-aware chunker.
2. Enrich each Data Act chunk with catalog metadata.
3. Produce atomic canonical citations at Article, paragraph, and point level.
4. Generate `CorpusMetadataV2.from_excerpt(...)` only after the final excerpt and citation are fixed.
5. Reject Data Act records with missing or unsupported v2 metadata rather than publishing them through the legacy path.

Exit criterion: every chunk has one instrument identity, one version, one canonical citation, and deterministic record/evidence IDs.

### Phase 3 — Export

1. Export the new records in the current JSON record envelope (`id`, `document`, `metadata`) so the existing adapter can load them.
2. Preserve all existing legacy records unchanged during the first mixed-corpus publication.
3. Append or merge Data Act records using deterministic source ordering.
4. Publish to a candidate artifact first; do not overwrite the working store until validation succeeds.
5. Record corpus counts, citation counts, checksums, and build version in a manifest.

Exit criterion: the candidate store contains readable legacy records plus complete metadata-v2 Data Act records, with no ID collisions.

### Phase 4 — Validate and publish

Run automated structural, identity, retrieval, and demo checks against the candidate artifact. Compare the existing recruitment demo output before and after corpus addition. Publish only if legacy evidence IDs and expected AI Act output are unchanged.

## 6. Validation gates

### 6.1 Corpus validation

Validate that:

- every Data Act record resolves to catalog instrument `EU_DATA_ACT`;
- every Data Act record declares schema version `2.0.0`;
- required v2 metadata is present and parseable by `CorpusMetadataV2`;
- `authority_level` is `binding_legislation`;
- canonical citations follow one normalized format;
- recomputed stable IDs equal exported stable IDs;
- `source_record_id` and `stable_evidence_id` values are unique within the build;
- identical inputs reproduce identical IDs across two builds;
- no excerpt is empty or crosses an Article boundary; and
- legacy records remain loadable without v2 metadata.

### 6.2 Citation retrieval verification

Create a small, legally reviewed retrieval test matrix that includes:

- the definitions of `connected product`, `related service`, `data holder`, and `user`;
- provisions concerning data generated by connected products and related services;
- user and third-party access provisions; and
- at least one negative/non-existent citation.

For each reference, assert:

1. retrieval is restricted to `EU_DATA_ACT`;
2. exact canonical matches are preferred;
3. returned excerpts contain the expected provision;
4. result ordering is deterministic; and
5. an AI Act or GDPR record is never returned for the same Article number.

The final citation matrix should be confirmed by legal review against the official text rather than inferred solely from parser output.

### 6.3 Evidence conversion verification

For every retrieved Data Act record, assert that the resulting `Evidence` object uses:

```text
legal_source       = EU_DATA_ACT
document_version   = record document_version
citation           = record canonical_citation
authority_level    = binding_legislation
evidence_id        = record stable_evidence_id
excerpt            = record document text
```

Also test a mixed result set containing Data Act v2 records and AI Act legacy records. The v2 records must use stable metadata identities while the legacy records retain their current evidence IDs and conversion behavior.

## 7. Industrial AI demo end-to-end verification

Use `tests/fixtures/industrial_ai_case.json` as the demonstration scenario. The test should wire the existing `EU_DATA_ACT_RELEVANCE` rule into an assessment workflow configured with the candidate corpus retriever.

Expected assessment behavior:

- the facts produce `EU_DATA_ACT_RELEVANCE`;
- framework is `EU_DATA_ACT`;
- category is `DATA_GOVERNANCE`;
- status is `potentially_applies`; and
- the language remains a preliminary relevance finding, not a compliance conclusion.

Expected evidence/report behavior:

- the finding receives Data Act evidence for its declared legal basis;
- every binding references an existing finding and Evidence ID;
- Evidence uses v2 instrument, version, citation, authority, and stable ID metadata;
- the report preserves Fact -> Finding -> Evidence traceability;
- serialization is deterministic across repeated runs; and
- no missing Data Act fact is introduced for the complete fixture.

The existing recruitment assessment must also be run as a regression test. Its finding, legal basis, evidence count, and legacy evidence IDs should remain unchanged.

## 8. Recommended implementation sequence

1. **Source and manifest:** acquire, checksum, parse, and manually verify the official text.
2. **Data Act chunk build:** generate structural chunks and complete v2 metadata in a separate candidate artifact.
3. **Automated validation:** test citations, schema completeness, ID determinism, uniqueness, and legacy compatibility.
4. **Retriever verification:** retrieve Data Act provisions and validate exact Evidence conversion.
5. **Workflow verification:** run the Industrial AI end-to-end scenario and the recruitment regression demo.
6. **Controlled publication:** replace or merge the production corpus only after all gates pass and retain the prior artifact for rollback.

This sequence keeps the new corpus isolated until it proves compatible with both the metadata-v2 Evidence path and the existing legacy AI Act retrieval path.
